# A 5-Year Plan Template for Young Ghanaians

Ask a young person where they see themselves in five years and you'll get a dream: "big business", "my own house", "finish school and be comfortable". Dreams are wonderful. But a five-year plan is not a dream with better phrasing — it's a dream **with a building schedule**.

I work with dozens of Klagon youth every week. The ones who are two years into real progress didn't have more talent than the ones who stalled. They had a document. A plan gives you something a wish can't: a decision filter, a re-reading bearing, and proof — when you feel lost — that you actually have a direction.

Here's the template I hand out at workshops. Fill it in this weekend. It takes one hour and it will outlive most resolutions.

## The 5-part template

### Part 1 — Destination (one clear sentence)

> In five years, I want to be [specific role or result] earning or living by [specific marker].

A good destination is *checkable*. "I want to be a graphic designer earning from my own clients in Tema" beats "I want to be successful" — because at year five you can tick it or not. Write one sentence. If you could write fifty, choose the one you'd be proudest to read back when times get tough.

### Part 2 — Year-by-year milestones

Break the destination into steps you can chase in 12-month chunks:

| Year | Milestone (something checkable) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | Skill foundation + first small wins | Finish 2 free courses; land first paying client; save GHS 500 |
| Year 2 | First real money + proof | 5 repeat clients or a steady weekly income; build a portfolio |
| Year 3 | Upgrade or scale | Bigger market, better tools, mentor, or a team of one apprentice |
| Year 4 | Consolidation | Consistent monthly target hit for 6 straight months |
| Year 5 | Arrival + next base | Destination reached; and write your *next* five-year plan |

Each milestone must be **checkable** (a number, a document, a title) — not a feeling.

### Part 3 — Skills you must gain (this is the real plan)

List the plain skills between you and the destination:

- E.g., client work → digital marketing, pricing, communication.
- E.g., business → bookkeeping, inventory, supplier negotiation.
- E.g., tech career → English for interviews, a tool (AI basics, Canva, Excel), project management.

Then assign a **start quarter** to each skill. "Learn AI basics by Q1" is a plan; "learn AI stuff one day" is a dream. Free material is everywhere — including the KlagonOrg Learning Hub's career, AI, and business courses.

### Part 4 — The money maths (honest numbers)

Plans fail when money is a mystery. Write two lines:

- **What this plan will cost me**: courses where needed, tools (data, transport, materials) — estimate per year.
- **How the plan pays for itself**: income milestones per year that cover it. A realistic plan is self-funding within two years: year 1 costs, year 2 learns to pay year 3.

If the plan can't feed itself by year 3, adjust the destination or the milestones — a five-year plan you can finance is stronger than one you can only admire.

### Part 5 — Weekly habits (the engine)

The whole plan runs on small repeats. Choose three habits only:

- 3 hours weekly on the current skill (that's a course completed every few weeks).
- 1 hour weekly on money (track spending, review income).
- 1 day weekly committed to the plan's biggest next step — no exceptions.

Three habits, not thirty. A five-year plan survives by being *boring enough to repeat*.

## How to review it so it doesn't die

A plan is alive only if you look at it.

- **Weekly (10 min, Sunday):** scroll the milestones. Which quarter am I in? What's this week's step?
- **Quarterly (1 hour):** check each milestone honestly. Behind? Adjust the *schedule*, not the dream. Ahead? Accelerate.
- **Yearly (2 hours):** rewrite the plan. Plans that don't change are no longer plans — they're prisons.

## A real example

One member of our program, a waakye-seller's daughter, wrote: *"In 5 years, earn GHS 3,000/month from digital design while running a small shop."* Her milestones were tame per year: learn the tool, do 3 free jobs for local shops, get paid, get 5 clients, then open the shop. Two years later she has paying clients and a waiting list — not because the plan was magical, but because every "boring Sunday" gave her the next small step while friends scrolled.
