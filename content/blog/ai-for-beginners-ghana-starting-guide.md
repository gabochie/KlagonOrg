# AI for Beginners in Ghana: A Free 15-Minute Starting Guide

There's a lot of noise about AI. Most of it is either doomsday talk or hype.

Let's strip it down to what a *beginner* in Ghana actually needs: what AI is, which free tools to try today, and the simple prompt formula that makes them useful.

## What AI actually is (no jargon)

The AI you'll meet first is called a **large language model** — that's a fancy way of saying:

> A computer trained on a huge amount of text that predicts the most likely next word, well enough to understand you and answer like a helpful assistant.

It's not magic. It's not conscious. It's a *tool* — like a calculator, but for words and questions. It can draft, explain, summarise, and brainstorm. It can also be confidently wrong, so you stay in charge of the answers.

## 3 free tools to try today

| Tool | What it's good for | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** (chatgpt.com) | General assistant: drafting, explaining, brainstorming | Free account, internet |
| **Google Gemini** (gemini.google.com) | Same, deeply tied to Google tasks | Free |
| **Microsoft Copilot** (copilot.microsoft.com) | Drafting, office-style work | Free |

One phone, an internet connection, and 15 minutes — that's the full setup. No laptop required.

## The prompt formula that works

People think prompting AI is a mystical art. It's a 4-part formula:

1. **Role:** who should it be? *"You are a career advisor for students in Accra."*
2. **Task:** what should it do? *"Write 3 interview questions for a mobile-money job."*
3. **Context:** what should it know? *"The student is 19, finished SHS, no experience."*
4. **Format:** how should it answer? *"List them numbered, with a one-line tip under each."*

Full example:
> *"You are a career advisor for students in Accra. Write 3 interview questions for a mobile-money job. The student is 19, finished SHS, no experience. List them numbered, with a one-line tip under each."*

That one-formula habit instantly upgrades your results from "meh" to genuinely useful.

## The one rule to keep in your pocket

**"Put me in charge" rule:** AI is a smart first draft, never the final word.

- For facts (prices, history, laws) — check a second source. AI makes things up sometimes.
- For money — never give it your money details or passwords.
- For decisions — you decide. AI proposes; you dispose.

## What to do with your next 15 minutes

1. Open ChatGPT (or Gemini/Copilot) on your phone.
2. Copy the sample prompt above and run it.
3. Rewrite it once, changing the **"context"** line to your own situation.
4. Ask it a follow-up: *"Make the questions harder."* Watch how giving feedback steers the answer.

That one session is your *first step* into digital transformation — using a powerful digital tool for a real task.
