# From Idea to First Sale: A Practical 30-Day Launch Plan

Most businesses never make a first sale — not because the idea failed, but because the *pace* was wrong. They aimed for a perfect product in nine months and never launched anything at all.

The antidote is ruthless speed: **a 30-day sprint to your first customer.** Here's the plan, week by week.

## Day 1–7: Sharpen the idea

### Refine to one sentence
"An app for education" is fog. "Group tutoring that fits school hours, priced per term, in my town" is sharp. Write your offer as one sentence a customer could understand.

### Find the painful core
Ask: *What's the single most painful problem your customer has, that you can actually solve?* Your product only needs to solve THAT well. Everything else is scope creep.

### Know your first 10 targets
Name the first 10 people who might buy — schools, churches, small workshops, neighbours. If you can't name 10, you don't have a market yet; keep talking to people until you can.

## Day 8–14: Build the minimum version

### Create the "minimum lovable" version
Build the smallest thing that proves the value: one service, one bundle, one product line — not the full catalogue. A great single thing beats five average ones.

### Set the starter price
Charge for value, not effort. A trial month discount is fine; giving it away free teaches you nothing. **A small paid first sale is a better teacher than thirty free "users."**

### Prepare 3 proof items
Gather: one before/after sample, one testimonial-shaped proof of your expertise, and one clean offer page (a simple WhatsApp message works).

## Day 15–21: Get in front of people

### Pitch the first 10 (the scary, necessary week)
Message, call, or walk in to your 10 targets. Script something like: *"I'm launching a coaching/hack offering. I want your honest opinion, and if it's useful, your first order at a launch price."* Most first sales come from direct, personal asks — not ads.

### Learn from every no
A "no" is data: *why not?* Collect the reasons. Adjust the offer each week. Fast, cheap failure in week 3 beats expensive failure in month 12.

### Async the tape: show the proof
Every positive response becomes a new proof item for the next person you approach. Each sale builds the confidence — and the story — for the next ten.

## Day 22–30: Close and deliver

### Close the first 3 sales
Don't chase 100. Three early customers are your launch. Ask each for the specific problem they want solved, then over-deliver on exactly that.

### Over-deliver visibly
Finish early. Give a small unexpected bonus. Be the version of "surprisingly good" people talk about. Your reputation spreads through the delivery, not the marketing.

### Capture the second-generation proof
After delivery, ask: *"Was that useful? Would you tell one friend?"* A real customer word beats every ad you could craft. Collect it now, while it's fresh.

## Your week-by-week scorecard

| Week | Win definition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nailed one-sentence offer + 10 targets named |
| 2 | Minimum version built + price set |
| 3 | First 10 pitched, feedback collected |
| 4 | First 3 sales closed and delivered |

That's it. Thirty days, four wins, one launched business.

## The honest truth about the sprint

The 30-day version will feel rough. That's the *point*: rough-but-launched beats polished-and-never.
