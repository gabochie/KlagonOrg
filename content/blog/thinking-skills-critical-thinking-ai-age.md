# Thinking Skills for the AI Age: Critical Thinking Is Your Career Superpower

There's a rumour going around that AI will make thinking unnecessary.

It's exactly backwards.

AI made *fast* thinking cheap — the kind of thinking Sara types a question and reads an answer instantly. But *slow* thinking — asking whether the answer is right, checking the assumptions, deciding what actually matters — is more valuable than ever, because **someone has to be responsible for the answers.**

That someone is you.

## Fast thinking vs slow thinking

Psychologists describe two mental gears:

- **Fast thinking:** automatic, instant, gut-level. Great for when you see a pot about to boil over.
- **Slow thinking:** deliberate, effortful, questioning. The kind of thinking that asks, "Is the AI hallucinating right now?"

The digital age is drowning us in fast answers. Your career will be built on your slow thinking — and the good news is, like a muscle, **it can be trained.**

## The 5 questions of a critical thinker

You don't need to be a philosopher to think critically. You need a handful of habits. These five questions cover most of the real decisions you'll face:

1. **What is actually being claimed?** Strip away clever words. "This product will change your life" means "buy this product."

2. **What is the evidence?** Ask: is it a fact, a story, or an opinion? A single story is a sample size of one — interesting, but not proof.

3. **Where is the bias?** Everyone (including you) has bias. Ask what the source gains from you believing this. An ad has a different agenda than a teacher.

4. **What matters and what doesn't?** Most decisions hinge on a few key facts. Identify them. The rest is noise.

5. **What's the downside if I'm wrong?** Weigh what happens on a mistake. Sometimes "good enough now" beats "perfect later."

## Three traps of the AI age — and how to escape them

AI didn't invent bad thinking. It automated it.

- **The acceptance trap:** you treat AI output as truth because it "sounds confident." Escape: demand the AI show its sources — and check one.
- **The shortcut trap:** you outsource an entire task to AI without understanding it. Escape: ask it to *explain how it got that answer* before you use it.
- **The echo-echo trap:** your feed shows you more of what you already believe. Escape: deliberately read one source you expect to disagree with, monthly.

## A 60-second thinking routine

When a big decision (or a big AI output) arrives, do this before you commit:

1. Pause. Type the decision out — one sentence.
2. Name one assumption you're making.
3. Name one person who'd disagree with you.
4. Ask: what would make me change my mind?

That's it. Four steps. That routine is the difference between being *reactive* and being *deliberate* — between letting the digital age happen to you and directing it.
