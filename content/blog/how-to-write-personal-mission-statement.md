# How to Write a Personal Mission Statement (With a Ghanaian Example)

A vision tells you *where* you're going. A mission statement tells you *why you exist as you head there.*

Big companies have them. Sports teams have them. But the most useful mission statement is the one most people never write: their own.

Done right, it's a one-line compass that makes dozens of daily decisions easier — because suddenly you know what deserves your yes and what deserves your no.

## What a personal mission statement is (and isn't)

- **It's NOT** a list of goals. Goals change every year; a mission stays stable for a decade.
- **It's NOT** a mood. "I want to be great" is a feeling, not a decision.
- **It IS** a one or two-sentence statement of **who you serve, what you do, and what you value.**

## The 3-part formula

> I [do something valuable] **for** [the people I serve] **so that** [the difference it makes].

That structure covers the three things a mission needs: **action, audience, impact.**

## Fill-in-the-blanks worksheet

Do these in order, out loud where possible:

1. **Action — what do you naturally do well?**
   - write stories · explain things · fix things · organise people · build with your hands · teach · create videos
   - Choose the one that energises you, not just the one you're "good at."

2. **Audience — who needs it most?**
   - students in my community · young people starting businesses · people learning to use their phones · my extended family
   - Pick the group you feel pulled to help.

3. **Impact — what changes because you did it?**
   - they get clear about their next step · they earn more money · they feel less afraid of technology · they find direction

Now slide your answers into the formula.

## A real example

Here's how Adjoa, a 22-year-old from Klagon, filled it in:

| Field | Her words |
|---|---|
| Action | I teach and simplify technology |
| Audience | for young people and small traders in my community |
| Impact | so that they can use digital tools to earn and save more |

**Her mission statement:**
> *"I teach and simplify technology for young people and small traders in my community, so that they can use digital tools to earn and save more."*

Simple. Readable. Her. Every choice — which skill to learn, what to post, which course to finish — now has one line to answer against.

Nothing fancy, yet that single sentence will quietly guide her for the next decade.

## Where to keep it (and use it)

- **Keep it:** your phone note, above your desk, in your planner. It belongs somewhere you'll see weekly.
- **Use it as a filter:** When a new opportunity appears, ask *"Does this move my mission forward?"* A "no" stops wasting your energy.
- **Review it yearly:** your mission can evolve as life does. That's healthy. Rewrite it, don't discard it.

## Pair it with your vision plan

A mission statement works brilliantly with the **personal development plan** — the mission is the *why*, the plan is the *how*. Together they form the most valuable page you'll ever write.

You can also workshop your mission with a supportive community — talking it through with mentors or peers often surfaces the "audience" you didn't even know mattered to you.

Write yours this week. One sentence. Your compass.
