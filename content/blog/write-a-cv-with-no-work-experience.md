# How to Write a CV With No Work Experience

"I can't apply — I don't have experience."

That sentence has cost more Ghanaians their first job than any other. Here's the truth employers operate on: **nobody hiring for an entry role expects you to have work experience.** They expect to see signals — proof that you show up, learn, and do. You've been sending signals your whole life; you just haven't been putting them on paper.

This guide rewires your CV so it stops being a list of gaps and becomes an argument for your potential.

## The one trick: your CV is a sales page, not a biography

Employers scan a CV in under ten seconds, looking for one answer: *"Could this person do the job and not embarrass me?"*

So structure everything toward that. No long paragraphs, no "I am a hardworking, God-fearing, dedicated person" opening (they've read it 200 times and believe none of it). Lead with what you've *done*, not what you *are*.

## The structure that works for first-timers

Use this order — the reverse of the old-school "personal details first" format:

1. **Header** — Name, phone, email, WhatsApp number, town. One line, nothing more. (No photo unless the job explicitly asks; no full house address, no date of birth, no religion, no marital status — those invitations to bias add nothing.)
2. **Professional summary (2–3 lines)** — Not "hardworking individual". Instead: *"SHS graduate and freelance Canva designer with 6 completed school and church projects; seeking an entry marketing role where I can support a busy team."* That tells the reader exactly what you can do.
3. **Education** — Your school, course/level, year, and any honours (e.g., WASSCE aggregate). Keep it tight.
4. **Projects and what you built** — THIS is your experience. School projects, church banners, a friend's shop flyers, a YouTube channel, a WhatsApp marketing stint you did for a family business. For each: *what you did, for whom, and the result*.
5. **Volunteering and community** — Real C.V. gold. Volunteering shows up, reliability, and teamwork. "Volunteer, KlagonOrg tree-planting drive" or "Usher, church convention" are honest experience lines.
6. **Skills** — A short grid, no full stops: Canva, WhatsApp business, cash handling, Microsoft Word, driving licence (if relevant), English & Twi written. Match the job advert's words.
7. **Languages and references** — "References available on request" is fine; also list languages you work in.

Nothing invented. But note what just happened: you went from "zero experience" to a page full of *evidence*.

## Turn anything into an experience line

Use the **Action + Result** formula: what you did, then what happened.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Helped in my family shop" | "Managed weekend sales and cash at family provision shop; counted stock and reported shortages weekly" |
| "Designs church things" | "Design banners and announcements for church; deliver 2 designs weekly within 24 hours" |
| "School project" | "Led a 5-member team in a marketing project; increased fundraiser turnout by ~40% within a week" |

Volunteering *is* all three of the things employers want: showing up, learning fast, and carrying tasks through. It's the closest thing to paid work you can legally put on paper — and in a country where most small businesses grew from relationships, community work is also how references happen. That's why the KlagonOrg volunteer projects are a serious first line on a serious first CV.

## The five rules that make or break a first CV

1. **One page.** Every first-timer's CV should fit one page. Cut until it does.
2. **Spellcheck it repeatedly.** One spelling error kills a first impression faster than no experience.
3. **Match the advert.** If they asked for "WhatsApp marketing", your skills column and summary must use those exact words. Employers search.
4. **No long sentences.** Bullets, verbs that start "-ed" or "-ing" (managed, designed), numbers where possible.
5. **Name the file properly.** `kwame-mensah-cv.docx`, not `cv-final-FINAL(2).pdf`. It shows organisation before the interview.

## The 15-minute version

If you apply tonight and have nothing written, do this now:

- Open a notes app. Write your skills (5 items), your biggest project or volunteer role (2 lines each), and your education line.
- Turn those into the 7 sections above.
- Exchange a photo for two proof lines in your summary. Send.

That's a real first CV. It won't be beautiful, but it will be *readable* — and readable beats perfect-but-empty, always.

## What to do after you send

Sending a CV is the start, not the finish:

- **Follow up once** in person or by WhatsApp after 4–5 days. "I sent my CV on Monday for the shop assistant role — happy to come for an interview anytime." Most applicants are ghost; a polite follow-up is a memory.
- **Keep building the portfolio.** Two more volunteer roles or projects a year keeps the CV evergreen.

You have never "had no experience". You've had *unwritten* experience. On paper — with evidence, structure, and one honest summary — you become the applicant who looks like they're going somewhere. And first-year employers hire *that* more than any certificate.
