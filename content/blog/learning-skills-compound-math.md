# How Learning Skills Compounds: The Math of Becoming Remarkable

Most people assume careers are built by being *better* than everyone at one thing. That's the **expertise myth** — and it's only half the story.

The fuller picture is about combining skills. You don't need to be in the top 1% of anything. You need to be in the top 25% of a few things that *work together.*

That's skill stacking — and it's the most under-used formula in the career world.

## The maths of combination

Imagine three skills, and you're at the 70th percentile in each (good, not great):

- Writing at 70% + AI knowledge at 70% = exceptional at "AI-assisted clear communication"
- Tech at 70% + business at 70% = exceptional at "building things that sell"
- Communication at 70% + leadership at 70% = exceptional at "organising people to move"

Individually, each skill is *unremarkable.* Combined, they create a rare intersection few people occupy — and rarity is what the market pays for.

**Remarkable is rarely about one skill. It's about a stack.**

## The three types of stacking

### 1. The classic stack (complementary)
Learn skills that multiply each other: marketing + a craft, AI + writing, finance + business. Each new layer makes every existing layer worth more.

### 2. The adaptability stack (adjacent)
Add skills one ring away from what you know: a mechanic learning electrics, a designer learning prompt engineering, a teacher learning data. This protects you when a layer shifts.

### 3. The insight stack (cross-domain)
Take a metaphor from one world into another. Sports taught Jeff Bezos; chess teaches generals. Learning something *completely* different often improves your original skill in surprising ways — reach out.

## Why stacking beats the "one talent" race

- **It's reachable.** Top 1% in one thing is about a 10-year war and considerable luck. Top 25% in three things is achievable in months — with consistent effort.
- **It's future-proof.** If one layer gets automated (say, plain writing by AI), your *stack* — AI + judgement + audience — stays relevant while the single-skill people scramble.
- **It's legal in ways talent isn't.** The skill stack is constructed, not born. That's good news for everyone who was never "gifted."

## The compounding habit

Skills compound fastest when learning is *daily and visible.* A consistent 20 minutes compounds better than occasional heroics, because compounding rewards *time in the game* more than any single effort.
