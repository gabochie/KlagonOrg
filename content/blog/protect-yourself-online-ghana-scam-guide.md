# Protect Yourself Online in Ghana: The Scam Guide You Need

"Auntie, the MTN office called me — they said I won a cash prize and just need my 24-hour passcode."

If you or someone you love has heard something like that, read carefully. Online scams in Ghana have moved from fake long-lost-uncle emails to calls targeting your mobile-money PIN, your bank OTP, and your goodwill.

The good news: scams run on predictable scripts. Once you know the scripts, you become very hard to trick. This guide covers the most common scams in Ghana right now and the pocket rules that keep your money and data safe.

## The scams that target Ghanaians most right now

**1. The "prize" call.** A stranger calls or WhatsApps, claiming you've won an MTN/Telecel/AT prize or lottery. To "receive" it, they need your 24-hour code, your MoMo PIN, or "a small processing fee". No company gives away money by asking you to *send* money or share security codes. Real prizes don't demand your PIN.

**2. Verification-code theft.** Someone calls pretending to be from your bank or mobile-money provider and "needs the code we just sent you to resolve an issue". That code is a one-time log-in or transfer authorization. Anyone who asks for your code is a thief. No legitimate institution ever asks for it — ever.

**3. Marketplace prepayment (the "goods" scam).** You find a good deal on Facebook Marketplace or TikTok — a phone, a console, an inflatable bouncy — and they want half-payment upfront "to reserve it". Then the account goes silent. Rule: never prepay strangers for goods you cannot collect in person or receive through a trusted courier.

**4. Fake loan apps.** Apps promising instant loans that ask for "processing fees" before disbursement, or demand dangerous phone permissions. Real lenders don't charge you to *borrow*. And granting app permissions over your contacts, photos, and messages hands scammers material to blackmail you with later.

**5. The romance or marriage scam.** A "foreigner" or "Ghanaian abroad" falls for you quickly, then needs money for airfare, visa, or an emergency. These are professional stories with professional patience. If someone you've never met in person asks for money, it's a block-and-report.

**6. Job and "work from home" offers.** "Earn GHS 500 a day from your phone — pay an activation fee to start." Real work pays *you*. Any job that requires money upfront, or will let you "chat with customers" while forwarding them money "for safekeeping", is a mule scam — you'll be the one the police trace.

## The four pocket rules

Keep these four on your phone's home-screen notes, and you'll survive almost any script:

1. **Never share codes.** PINs, OTPs, 24-hour codes, passwords — nobody legitimate asks. Your thumbnail can be interviewed for its memory of them.
2. **Never pay to receive.** Fees before prizes, loans, jobs, or "special deals" are the scam's signature.
3. **Verify before you trust.** Hang up, and call the official number from the *company's* website or receipt to confirm. Scammers happily pretend to be Fidelity, Vodafone, or MTN.
4. **Slow down.** Urgency is the scammer's engine. "Only today! Send now!" is designed to beat your thinking. Real opportunities survive a phone call to verify.

## Set up your phone so it protects you

- **Turn on two-step verification** for WhatsApp, Gmail, and your e-money apps. It's a settings toggle that blocks account theft.
- **Use the app, not the website**, for banking and MoMo — official apps are harder to fake than SMS links.
- **Block app permissions.** Reject camera, contacts, SMS, and microphone access for apps that don't need them. A 5-minute walk through your app settings closes most holes.
- **Update your apps.** Old versions have known holes. Updates are the patch; ignoring them is leaving your door ajar.
- **Use the official app stores**, and check the developer name before installing. A QR code pasted in a WhatsApp group is not a safe install.

## What to do if you've been scammed

Act fast, without shame — scammers are professionals, and victims are not guilty:

1. **Block the number** and report it on WhatsApp and Truecaller-style apps (if available).
2. **Contact your mobile-money provider and bank immediately** to freeze accounts and reverse pending transactions.
3. **Change your passwords and PINs** right away.
4. **Report to the police or Cyber Crime Unit** and keep all chats and transactions as evidence. Numbers like the National Cyber Security Centre's helpline may help guide you.
5. **Tell someone.** A victim who stays silent feeds the next victim down the line. Sharing the script you fell for genuinely protects your community.
